## Said

*Observation, 2026-09-01: Buzz Usborne, "AI Operating Principles"
(buzzusborne.com/writing/ai-operating-principles, August 2026) — a
published, bylined essay, cited rather than pseudonymized, per the
playbook-entry precedent. Read via summary fetch; James flagged it for
the record. Five numbered principles for a designer working with AI.*

№1, the why matters more than the how:

> "AI should challenge and strengthen that reasoning, but never replace
> it."

> "Protect your ability to explain why something exists, what you
> believe and what evidence changed your mind."

№2, stay in the unknown:

> "Design often requires us to remain uncomfortable long enough to
> understand why the obvious answer isn't right."

№3, earn the right to slow down:

> "Speed in one area creates the capacity to spend disproportionate time
> on the opportunities that deserve it."

№4, humanity balances logic:

> "The weird, warm and disproportionately considered details will become
> evidence that a real person donated part of their soul."

№5, beware of normalizing complexity:

> "AI dramatically reduces the cost of creating complexity without
> reducing the consequences of maintaining it."

> "AI should remove work, not create supervision."

On workflow: "Remove the distance between design and engineering."

## Meant

A working designer publishing operating principles for AI-assisted
practice — the genre itself is a signal that the field feels the need to
codify discipline around speed. Unverified beyond the essay's text.

The resonances with Canon are close enough to note. №1's "what evidence
changed your mind" is the decision log's exact job, and the Cairn
candidate in one sentence. №3 is the record's economic argument: speed
spent on building buys the room to keep evidence properly. №5 is the
deter position restated — reviews that remove, additions that must
survive scrutiny — and an argument that a feedback tool must not become
"supervision." One voice; none of this pools with the thread
participants.
