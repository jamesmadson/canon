## Said

*Public LinkedIn post by a design-agency partner who has shipped
design-in-code for nine products, 2026-08-27 capture. Excerpts:*

> "We need the final output in code, not in Figma". We hear this more and
> more often as a product design agency.

> doing product design this way needs a different process every time.
> Depending on the product stage, the setup a product already has and the
> limitations around it.

> We put a design engineer inside the team who actually builds the right
> design system. That system becomes the common source everything else is
> generated from.

> the most efficient tools get banned for security reasons. … for some
> products the tool that is secure enough can be not efficient enough.

> the AI that was meant to speed the work up can become the bottleneck
> that slows down the team. And without a common design source and a
> clear new process, it can end up hurting the quality of the product.

## Meant

Practitioner confirmation from the delivery side, not the tooling side.
Three readings: (1) "a common source everything else is generated from"
is the `system` contextual skill stated as an agency's billable practice —
second independent voice for that concept after the shared-surface essay.
(2) Stage segmentation — early-stage easiest, enterprise hardest on
tool-approval grounds — sharpens both the PRD's "who it is for" and the
repo-trust assumption; enterprise security review is a wall, not a
speed bump. (3) The client demand quote ("final output in code, not in
Figma") is the market's version of the thread's Figma demotion pattern.
Unverified: which nine products, and how much of the process is
proprietary versus generalizable.
