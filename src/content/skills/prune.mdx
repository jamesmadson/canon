Ask an agent to review an overdesigned screen and it often writes a capable
critique, then buries it: twenty findings across five frameworks, graded with
coloured icons, additions mixed in with removals, and a rewritten component
nobody asked for.

A Prune review has five parts and nothing else: the surface's **job** in one
sentence, **removals** each priced as a cost to that job, at least one thing
**kept** with its reason, at most one **missing** piece, and the **smallest
next step**. How often people meet the surface multiplies every cost. A
flourish on a screen people use daily costs its price every day.

- **Findings, not files.** It writes no revised version unless you ask after
  reading the review.
- **A review that keeps nothing has not understood the design.** The kept
  section is required.
- **Taste needs a mechanism.** "Gradients feel dated" is not a finding. "The
  gradient band pushes the renewal date below the fold on a card checked
  daily" is.
- **No severity scores.** The order is the ranking, and the reason is the
  finding.

Where a project keeps a Canon record, such as Strata's `.strata/` folder,
Prune checks its removals against it first and cites the entries it uses. A
decision the team already recorded outranks the skill's defaults.

Canon's own, MIT, installable from the Canon marketplace.
