Not another motion checklist — a panel. The skill distills the published work of three motion designers into three attributable lenses that each answer a different question: Emil Kowalski asks *"should this animate at all?"*, Jakub Krehel asks *"is this subtle and polished enough for production?"*, and Jhey Tompkins asks *"what could this become?"* The sharp idea is that the lenses are weighted by project context before anything else happens — a productivity tool leads with Emil, a kids' app leads with Jakub and Jhey — which turns "it depends" from a shrug into an actual lookup table.

The frequency gate is the hardest-working rule in it: rare interactions earn expressive motion, daily ones get subtle-and-fast, hundred-times-a-day ones get nothing, and keyboard-initiated actions never animate. Two modes — Create builds components with purposeful motion, Audit reviews existing motion and emits a self-contained HTML report with looping demos of each finding.

There's a meta angle for Canon readers: two of the three distilled designers are already in this gallery first-hand (Emil's Improve Animations and Design Eng skills, Jakub's Make Interfaces Feel Better). This is the synthesis view — useful precisely when you want the voices weighed against each other rather than one philosophy applied pure.

**Install**: the repo bundles a `skills/` folder — copy `skills/design-motion-principles/` into your `.claude/skills/` directory (references and workflows come with it).

**Gotcha**: the author is explicit that the lenses are his interpretation of each designer's *published* work, "named in tribute; not authored or endorsed by the designers themselves." Honest framing, worth knowing before you cite a finding as "what Emil would say."
