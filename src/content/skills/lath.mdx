Lath is the thin strips under plaster: structure before finish. Ask for a
wireframe and it draws the screen from a closed kit of twelve parts, such as
a title bar, a field, a list row and a sheet, plus twelve more for chat and
app screens. Every wireframe looks like it came from the same hand, and you
argue about structure before anyone argues about colour.

Before drawing, it settles three things:

- **The format.** Phone, tablet or desktop, at a fixed size.
- **The copy.** Real words in the product's voice. When the brief leaves out
  a price, a plan name or a date, it writes a plausible one and adds a note
  saying it assumed it.
- **The states.** Loading, empty, error, success and any others that apply,
  listed beside every screen.

One primary action per view. Asked to revise, it makes the one change named
and leaves every other byte alone. Asked to annotate, it adds numbered pins
with matching notes, in the same orange-red Canon uses for comments, so a
wireframe note and a review comment read as one language.

Canon's own, MIT, installable from the Canon marketplace.
